Abstract 
Flight delays remain a persistent challenge in the aviation industry, causing financial losses for 
airlines, disrupting flight operations, and frustrating passengers. Existing delay management 
approaches depend largely on fixed schedules or manual decision-making, which are insufficient 
for handling the complexity of modern air traffic. Variables such as adverse weather, airport 
congestion, aircraft rotation, and crew availability create dynamic conditions that make delay 
forecasting difficult. This lack of accurate predictive systems leads to inefficiencies, reduced 
passenger trust, and operational strain on flight managers. Addressing this challenge is vital to 
improve airline efficiency, reduce costs, and enhance the overall passenger experience. 
This project, Skylytics – An AI Powered Flight Delay Prediction System, proposes an intelligent 
solution that integrates multiple data sources including historical flight records, real-time weather 
data, and airport information. Using a hybrid machine learning approach combining models such 
as XGBoost and LSTM/Transformer, the system will forecast both the likelihood and duration of 
delays. Explainable AI techniques (e.g., SHAP) will provide transparency by highlighting the key 
factors influencing each prediction. A web dashboard (Next.js, Tailwind) and an iOS mobile 
application (SwiftUI) will deliver user-friendly interfaces for both flight managers and passengers, 
offering features like what-if analysis, delay heatmaps, smart notifications, and an AI assistant. 
Cloud deployment with FastAPI and scalable databases will ensure fast, reliable, and professional 
performance, making Skylytics a practical and industry-relevant Final Year Project.

1. Introduction 
Air travel plays a vital role in modern global connectivity, supporting the movement of people, 
goods, and services while driving economic growth and cultural exchange. With millions of 
passengers depending on airlines daily, reliability and punctuality are critical. However, flight 
delays remain one of the most persistent challenges in aviation, creating cascading disruptions, 
financial losses, and passenger dissatisfaction. Airlines operate on tight schedules where a minor 
delay can ripple across multiple flights, while passengers often face missed connections, business 
losses, or added expenses. 
Globally, billions of dollars are lost annually due to delays. In the United States alone, thousands 
of flights are delayed each year by more than 15 minutes, as reported by the Bureau of 
Transportation Statistics (BTS). Causes range from adverse weather and air traffic congestion to 
crew scheduling and technical issues. These complexities make delay prediction a difficult but 
crucial task for improving aviation reliability. Traditional systems relying on manual assessments 
or static schedules are inadequate, as they fail to process the vast, real-time data required to capture 
non-linear and interdependent delay factors. 
Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) provide a transformative solution. By 
leveraging massive datasets - including historical flight records, weather data, and airport 
congestion - AI models can detect hidden patterns and generate real-time predictions. This 
empowers airline operation managers to proactively address disruptions and enhances passengers’ 
trust in airline services. 
Skylytics is proposed as an AI-powered flight delay prediction system designed to address these 
challenges. By integrating data from BTS, OpenFlights, and Meteostat, the system will employ 
hybrid ML models (XGBoost, LSTMs, and Transformers) to predict both delay likelihood and 
duration. Beyond prediction, Skylytics emphasizes explainability and usability: managers will gain 
insights into “why” a flight is delayed through SHAP values, test alternate scenarios with what-if 
simulations, and collaborate with teams using built-in communication tools. Passengers, in turn, 
will access real-time updates, delay notifications, and monitoring features. 
This project represents more than delay forecasting - it envisions a holistic aviation management 
ecosystem. By fusing predictive analytics, explainable AI, and user-focused design, Skylytics aims 
to reduce uncertainty, optimize airline operations, and enhance passenger experience. For this 
Final Year Project (FYP), it offers both deep technical learning and real-world impact, setting a 
benchmark for applying AI to practical aviation challenges. 
 
 
 
 

2. Problem Description 
2. 1 Primary Scope 
The aviation industry faces one of its most persistent challenges in the form of flight delays, which 
impact both airline operators and passengers. The problem is not merely about late arrivals; it 
extends to cascading operational inefficiencies, increased financial costs, and significant passenger 
dissatisfaction. Despite decades of technological advancements, most existing delay management 
systems rely on static schedules, manual monitoring, or limited statistical models that are 
insufficient for capturing the dynamic and multi-factor nature of delays. 
Flight delays can be caused by diverse factors such as adverse weather, airport congestion, 
technical malfunctions, crew scheduling conflicts, and even cascading effects from earlier delayed 
flights. Each of these components interacts in non-linear ways, making manual prediction nearly 
impossible. Without reliable predictive tools, airline managers are left to reactively manage crises 
rather than proactively preventing them. 
This project seeks to answer three fundamental questions: 
• 
What is the problem? 
Persistent and costly flight delays that reduce reliability in aviation. 
• 
Why is it important? 
Because delays harm airlines financially, frustrate passengers, and reduce overall trust in air travel 
systems. 
• 
How can it be solved? 
By leveraging AI-driven predictive analytics, historical and real-time datasets, and explainable 
machine learning models to forecast flight delays accurately and provide actionable insights. 
By building an intelligent predictive system, this project addresses a pressing real-world challenge 
while contributing innovative solutions that align with global trends toward AI-enabled decision 
support systems in aviation. 
 
2. 2 Final Deliverables and Beneficiaries 
The product of this work will be a web-based platform and iOS application powered by an AI-
driven backend capable of predicting flight delays and providing actionable insights. 

• 
Direct beneficiaries: Airline operation managers, flight schedules, and ground staff who can 
make data-driven decisions to reduce operational inefficiencies. 
• 
Indirect beneficiaries: Passengers, who will receive more reliable updates and improved travel 
experiences, and airlines, which will reduce costs and protect their reputation. 
 
2. 3 Optional Scope 
If resources allow, additional premium integrations will be considered, such as real-time aviation 
data APIs (e. g., FlightAware, FlightRadar24) for higher prediction accuracy, and 3D flight 
visualization modules (via Three.js) to enhance user interactivity. While not mandatory, these 
features would significantly expand the usability and professional depth of the system. 
 
2. 4 Objectives 
1. The primary objective of this project is to develop a reliable and industry-ready flight delay 
prediction system that integrates multiple data sources such as flight schedules, weather 
information, and airport operational records. 
2. For the prototype phase, tree-based models such as XGBoost, Random Forest, and LightGBM 
will be used to establish the baseline system. 
3. In the advanced phase, the system will move to hybrid ensemble models that combine 
XGBoost with LSTM and Transformer architectures to predict both the likelihood and the 
duration of delays. 
4. Explainable AI techniques such as SHAP and LIME will be incorporated to ensure 
transparency and trust, allowing operational managers to clearly understand the factors 
influencing each prediction. 
5. The solution will include a professional and user-friendly interface in the form of a web-based 
dashboard, featuring interactive capabilities such as delay heatmaps, what-if analysis, and 
smart notifications. 
6. An iOS mobile application will also be developed to provide quick access to predictions and 
real-time monitoring for operational managers and staff. 
7. Performance optimization will be achieved through caching, load balancing, and efficient 
cloud deployment strategies, with hosting support provided by platforms such as AWS, GCP, 
and Vercel, along with Apple Developer accounts for iOS distribution. 
8. The system will be rigorously evaluated using established accuracy metrics such as ROC-
AUC, RMSE, and F1-score, and complemented with user experience testing to validate its 
effectiveness in real-world operational scenarios.

3. Literature Review 
3. 1 Flight Delay Prediction 
Flight delay prediction remains one of the most persistent challenges in the aviation industry. 
According to the U. S. Bureau of Transportation Statistics (BTS), nearly 20% of all flights 
experience delays annually, creating cascading effects for airlines, airports, and passengers [1]. 
Traditional methods of prediction relied heavily on regression models or historical averages [2]. 
These statistical tools, though useful for broad patterns, failed to capture the dynamic, multi-
factorial causes of delays such as weather, airport congestion, and aircraft rotations. 
With the increasing availability of large aviation datasets, researchers have shifted towards 
machine learning approaches. Rebollo and Balakrishnan [3] highlighted the insufficiency of static 
tools in fast-changing traffic conditions and advocated for data-driven models. These approaches 
allow more accurate and flexible prediction by incorporating diverse data sources. 
 
3. 2 Machine Learning Models Used in Delay Prediction 
Several ML models have been applied in flight delay prediction: 
• 
Decision Trees and Random Forest (RF): 
Random Forest has been widely adopted due to its interpretability and robustness. Xu et al. 
[4] demonstrated its strength in managing heterogeneous data types and nonlinear 
interactions in flight operations. 
• 
Gradient Boosting Models (XGBoost, LightGBM): 
Chen and Guestrin [5] introduced XGBoost, which has since become a standard in aviation 
prediction tasks. It efficiently handles missing values and complex interactions, making it 
suitable for binary tasks like predicting whether a flight will be delayed. 
• 
Deep Learning (LSTM, Transformers): 
LSTM models are particularly strong at handling sequential data, making them effective in 
modeling delay propagation from inbound flights [6]. More recently, Transformer-based 

approaches have been applied for aviation time-series forecasting, handling long 
dependencies with superior performance [7]. 
• 
Hybrid Approaches: 
Combining ensemble models (like XGBoost) with temporal models (LSTM/Transformer) 
improves prediction performance. Chen and Huang [8] showed that hybrid systems 
consistently outperform individual models, though they require more computational 
resources. 
 
3. 3 Explainability in AI for Aviation 
While machine learning models can achieve high accuracy, their adoption in aviation requires trust 
and transparency. Black-box systems are often resisted by airlines and regulators. Explainable AI 
(XAI) frameworks such as SHAP (SHapley Additive Explanations) and LIME provide insights 
into feature contributions [9]. 
In aviation delay prediction, this means managers can understand whether weather, traffic, or 
operational factors are most responsible for a predicted delay. While explainability has been 
widely explored in fields like finance and healthcare [10], its systematic use in aviation systems is 
still limited. Zhang et al. [12] demonstrated that providing feature attribution significantly 
improved operator confidence in delay prediction tools. 
 
3. 4 Gaps in Existing Research 
From current literature, the following gaps are clear: 
1. Lack of real-time data integration: Many models rely solely on static datasets and ignore 
live updates [11]. 
2. Low practical deployment: Few studies develop production-ready systems accessible to 
airlines. 
3. Explainability gap: Accuracy is prioritized over interpretability, limiting adoption [12]. 
4. Passenger-facing systems are rare: Most research is operations-focused. 
5. Scalability challenges: Scaling deep models to global datasets remains a bottleneck [13]. 
6. Simulation and what-if analysis: Almost absent in the reviewed literature [14]. 
 
3. 5 Summary 
The reviewed literature confirms the potential of machine learning in aviation but highlights 
critical shortcomings. Skylytics addresses these gaps by: 
• 
Using a hybrid ML model (XGBoost + LSTM/Transformer). 

• 
Integrating real-time data streams (weather + operations). 
• 
Applying XAI techniques (SHAP, LIME) to ensure transparency. 
• 
Building both manager- and passenger-facing interfaces. 
• 
Providing simulation, history tracking, and performance analytics. 
This dual focus on academic rigor and practical deployment ensures novelty and real-world 
impact.

4. Methodology 
The methodology of this project is structured as an iterative, end-to-end workflow that begins with 
data collection and preparation, advances into model development and optimization, and concludes 

with evaluation, deployment, and user-facing application design. To ensure technical soundness 
and practical usability, the process is divided into two phases: prototyping (establishing feasibility 
and baselines) and advanced development (building scalable, explainable, and production-ready 
models). 
 
Figure 1 
 
4. 1 Prototyping Phase 
The prototyping phase establishes the foundation of the system through simplified datasets and 
baseline models. This stage validates feasibility, sets benchmark metrics, and highlights potential 
challenges before investing in complex architectures. 
• 
Dataset Selection and Preprocessing 
Historical flight delay records from Kaggle and BTS form the initial dataset. Raw data is 
cleaned to address missing values, standardize time formats, and ensure logical consistency 
between scheduled and actual flights. Outliers are retained where they represent realistic 
events, such as severe weather. 
• 
Baseline Modeling 
Simple models such as Logistic Regression and Decision Trees are implemented to measure 

predictive potential. These benchmarks serve as a reference for evaluating improvements 
gained through more advanced models later. 
• 
Pipeline Validation 
The prototype includes exploratory data analysis (EDA), basic ETL scripts, and small-scale 
experiments in scikit-learn. This confirms that the system can process aviation datasets 
reliably and lays the groundwork for more complex workflows. 
 
4. 2 Advanced Model Development Phase 
Building on the validated prototype, this phase introduces advanced models, feature engineering, 
and system integration for real-world application. 
• 
Data Handling and Feature Engineering 
Multiple data sources including Meteostat (weather) and OpenFlights (airport routes) are 
integrated. Feature engineering introduces derived attributes such as congestion indices, day-
of-week encodings, and historical averages. Time-based features are encoded cyclically, 
categorical variables are machine-encoded, and scaling ensures consistency. 
                      
Figure 2          

• 
Ensemble and Sequential Models 
Gradient boosting models (XGBoost, LightGBM) capture nonlinear interactions. To address 
sequential dependencies, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Transformers are 
incorporated. Outputs from these models are fused into a hybrid ensemble, combining 
predictive accuracy with interpretability. 
 
 
Figure 3          
 
• 
Optimization and Validation 
Hyperparameter tuning (Randomized Search, Optuna) ensures performance efficiency. 
Cross-validation mitigates overfitting, while imbalanced classes are addressed through 
SMOTE and class reweighting. 
 
4. 3 Evaluation, Explainability, and Deployment 
Once trained, models are evaluated with multiple metrics, since accuracy alone is insufficient in 
imbalanced datasets. Precision, Recall, F1-Score, and ROC-AUC provide a multi-dimensional 
performance view. Misclassifications undergo error analysis to refine model design. 
Explainability tools such as SHAP and LIME ensure transparency by attributing predictions to 
features like weather or congestion. This improves trust and enables operational managers to 
understand model behavior. 

For deployment, FastAPI serves predictions through real-time APIs, with Docker containers 
ensuring portability. Redis provides caching for fast queries, and optional Kubernetes orchestration 
supports scalability. Monitoring pipelines detect data drift to maintain reliability. 
 
4. 4 User Interfaces and Applications 
The final stage emphasizes usability. 
• 
Web Dashboard (Next.js + Tailwind) for airline managers, featuring delay predictions, 
SHAP-based explanations, delay heatmaps, and what-if analysis tools. Collaboration features 
allow teams to share insights and coordinate responses. 
• 
iOS Mobile App (SwiftUI) for passengers, focusing on real-time predictions, monitoring, and 
push notifications. Both interfaces connect securely to the backend via APIs, ensuring 
consistency in results.

4. 5 Workflow Summary 
The methodology follows a two-stage evolution: 
1. Prototyping with historical datasets and baseline models to validate feasibility and set 
benchmarks. 
2. Advanced Development with integrated datasets, hybrid models, explainability, and 
production deployment for end-user applications. 
This staged approach ensures that the system matures from a feasible prototype into a robust, 
industry-ready solution that balances technical rigor, scalability, and usability.

5. 2 Risks Involved 
The proposed project involves some potential risks that need to be considered: 
1. Data Availability: Real-time APIs (e.g., Meteostat, OpenSky) may have usage limits or 
downtime.  
Mitigation: Use cached data and maintain fallback Kaggle datasets for continuity. 
2. Model Accuracy: Flight delay prediction is inherently uncertain due to unpredictable events 
(e.g., emergencies).  

Mitigation: Use hybrid models and explainable AI to improve trust and reliability. 
3. Compute Limitations: Training advanced models may require high processing power.  
Mitigation: Use cloud services (Google Colab Pro, AWS) for scalability. 
4. Integration Complexity: Multi-platform deployment (web + iOS) may create integration 
challenges.  
Mitigation: Follow modular development with API-first design. 
Overall, these risks are manageable and do not hinder the successful completion of the project.
